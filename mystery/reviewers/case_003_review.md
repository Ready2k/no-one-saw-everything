# Case 003: The Clinic After Hours — Executive Showrunner Review
*Review pass 2 — post-rebuild. Supersedes pass 1 (13 Jul), which issued **Promising but Needs Major Rewrite** at 6.5/10 — "one scene away." That scene, and the time-of-day fixes, are now in.*

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The best *premise* in the pack, and it now has the ending it was one scene short of. A district
nurse murders a beloved patient — not for money she would keep, but for a **clinic fund**: a
treatment room, equipment that could save lives. She poisons him with his own heart medication,
hands it to him herself, and then goes back to reception and stares at a file without reading it.
The "institutional" motive is still the most original idea in the project.

Pass 1's blocker was that Nadia never broke — her strongest challenge ended on a defiant "you
cannot prove that," and there were no epilogues, so the player assembled the case and was handed
nothing. Both are fixed. Her `contradiction_locked` is present and it is a *justification, not a
plea*: *"Eleven years of a broken autoclave and a waiting list… He offered. He sat in my chair and
he said, Nadia, when I'm gone you'll have your treatment room. And I built everything round it. The
five-year plan is in my handwriting."* The seven epilogues are the pack's most quietly furious —
the estate goes to a nephew who sells the cottage and does not come to the funeral, the clinic
receives nothing, and everyone in the village knows it.

The wrong-time-of-day artifacts pass 1 flagged as P0 were only partly closed; this pass found and
fixed the last two (Nadia opening "for the early shift" and Clara "opening the cafe" at 17:30 — a
16:00–18:00 case does not open a cafe at half five). Ben's routine is already corrected to an
afternoon round.

What holds it below case 001: the *means* proof against Nadia is necessarily circumstantial (she
is the one person with dispensary access, but a poisoning leaves the player convinced rather than
cornered), the lone red herring is thin, and the second suspect — Dr Haig — appears in *no rewind
events at all*, so a player who suspects him has nothing to investigate. That last one is the real
remaining defect.

## INDIVIDUAL REVIEWER REPORTS

### Agent 1 — The Detective

**Logic Score:** 7/10 (was 6) · **Fairness Score:** 7/10 · **Difficulty Score:** 6/10

**Assessment:** The opportunity case is airtight — Nadia has sole access to the locked dispensary
and the pill organiser, and the digoxin-into-the-organiser method is clean. The motive is fully
seeded (the bequest, the will change, the five-year plan in her hand). The confession now closes
the loop the "you cannot prove it" ending left open.

**Remaining issues:**

1. **HIGH CONFIDENCE — Dr Haig is a suspect with an empty rewind.** The validator flags
   `agent_dr_haig` as appearing in no events. He signs off Nadia's dispensing records and is the
   natural second suspect (medical access, authority) — but a player who follows that instinct into
   the Rewind or Map finds him nowhere on the morning. A red herring the player cannot *investigate*
   is not a red herring; it is a dead name. He needs at least one placed event (arriving to
   countersign, leaving before the window) so suspicion has somewhere to go and somewhere to die.
2. **MEDIUM CONFIDENCE — The means proof is conviction, not capture.** Poisoning cases inherently
   lack a caught-red-handed beat; the case leans on access + motive + the confession. That is fair,
   but one hard corroborating clue (a trace on the organiser, a dispensary log discrepancy) would
   move the player from "it must be her" to "it can only be her."

**Suggested fixes:** (1) Give Dr Haig 1–2 rewind events. (2) Add one physical dispensary clue. Both P1.

### Agent 2 — The Screenwriter

**Entertainment Score:** 7/10 · **Structure Score:** 8/10 (was 6)

**Tension curve:** A strong, unusual open — the victim is found slumped on a public bench in plain
view, and the horror is that *several people saw him and assumed he was asleep*. The middle is a
patient tightening around the one person with the keys. The climax now exists and it is a good one:
Nadia does not crumble, she *argues*.

**Weakest Act:** Act 2 — the red herring is too thin to sustain a real detour, so the middle is a
fairly direct line to Nadia.

**Strongest Act:** Act 3. The confession's refusal to be sorry is the case's signature, and the
epilogue about the nephew is a gut-punch coda.

**Highest ROI rewrite:** Build Dr Haig into a genuine second suspect (rewind presence + a plausible
motive of his own). It fixes the Detective's fairness note and the thin middle in one move.

**Would critics recommend it?** Yes; this is the case that most resembles prestige TV (*Broadchurch*,

### Agent 6 — Village Simulator

**Village realism score:** 7/10

The late-afternoon setting is now coherent after the time-of-day fixes — the square at going-home

## SHOWRUNNER SUMMARY

### CONSENSUS

All six confirm the confession and epilogues resolve pass 1's blocker, and the motive remains the
pack's most original. Three reviewers independently flagged **Dr Haig's absence from the rewind** —
the Detective as a fairness fault, the Player as a frustration, the Simulator as a presence gap.
When the puzzle specialist, the player proxy, and the world specialist land on the same name, it is
the priority.

### DISAGREEMENTS

The Screenwriter wants a richer middle; the Detective wants a firmer means proof. These are the same
fix seen from two angles: making Dr Haig a real, investigable second suspect adds a middle detour
*and* forces the player to actively rule him out, which sharpens the case against Nadia by contrast.

## EXECUTIVE DECISION

### **Good** — release-ready, one P1 (Dr Haig) short of pushing toward Excellent.

**Would I fund it?** Yes — for the premise alone.

**Would I release it today?** Yes. It is complete and fair. But I would prioritise the Haig fix in
the first patch window, because "the game forgot a suspect exists" is a review-thread complaint.

**Would I delay it?** No — ship, then patch Haig.

**Three highest-ROI improvements before launch:** (1) make Dr Haig investigable; (2) one hard means
clue; (3) a real stake for the red herring.

- Stand alongside the best modern detective games? **As drama, yes; as a puzzle, not quite.**
- Remembered after one week? **Yes.** · One month? **Yes — the motive.** · One year? **Possibly.**
- Recommend to friends? **Yes.** · Replay? **No.** · Streamers enjoy it? **Yes, for the debate.**

### "Why will players remember this mystery?"

Because it makes the killer's case *to their face* and half-convinces them. Most mysteries ask "who
did it"; this one asks "and was she wrong," and it does not answer. The treatment room that never
got built, the plan in her own handwriting, the nephew who sold the cottage — players will argue
about Nadia on the drive home, and the argument is the point. Close the one gap where the game
forgets Dr Haig is a suspect, and this sits a hair below the flagship.
