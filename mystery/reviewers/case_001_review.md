# Case 001: The Storage Room Murder – Executive Showrunner Review

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
This is a tight, classical locked-room-adjacent mystery that relies heavily on strict timeline mechanics and well-drawn, grounded motives. The mechanics of the crime are extremely solid: a tight murder window (07:45-08:00), a physical timeline that maps perfectly to the village layout, and a cast of characters who all have secrets but only one has the true motive and opportunity. The emotional core—Clara's desperation over her mother's care debts versus Marcus's cold ledgers—elevates the narrative above a mere logic puzzle. While the execution is strong, the primary weakness lies in its structural linearity and the fact that the killer's alibi breaks fairly easily if the player cross-references two specific witnesses.

## INDIVIDUAL REVIEWER REPORTS

### Agent 1 – The Detective
**Logic Score:** 9/10
**Fairness Score:** 9/10
**Difficulty Score:** 6/10
**Top Issues:** Clara’s alibi is very easily broken. Ben sees her coat in the alley, Elias confirms she wasn't at the fountain, and the damp coat in the kitchen clinches it. It's almost too neat.
**Suggested Fixes:** Obscure Clara's movement slightly or make the blue coat sighting more ambiguous. Perhaps Ben only saw a shadow, and the player has to deduce the coat from the damp sleeve alone.

### Agent 2 – The Screenwriter
**Entertainment Score:** 7/10
**Structure Score:** 8/10
**Tension curve:** Starts high with the public argument (Owen), dips during routine alibi checks, and spikes sharply when Priya confesses her secret meeting in the alley.
**Weakest Act:** The mid-game. Checking alibis against Elias's bench observations feels mechanical.
**Strongest Act:** The climax. Confronting Clara with the ledger, the debt, and the damp coat provides a very satisfying narrative closure.
**Highest ROI rewrite:** Deepen the Isabella red herring. Her note is great, but her alibi (clinic with Nadia) is airtight and easily verified, shutting down that tension too quickly.

## SHOWRUNNER SUMMARY

### TOP STRENGTHS
1. **The Tragic Motive:** Clara stealing to pay care home fees is grounded, understandable, and heartbreaking. It avoids cartoonish villainy.
2. **The "Blue Coat" Sighting:** The way Ben and Priya's innocent secret meeting intersects with Clara's guilt is excellent structural writing.
3. **Physical Evidence Integration:** The till weight going from a mundane object to the missing weapon hidden in the mop bucket is classic fair-play design.
4. **The Ledger:** A perfect inciting incident that forces the timeline.
5. **Background Routines:** The background agents (milkman, postman) make the village feel incredibly alive.

### TOP WEAKNESSES
1. **Alibi Fragility:** The red herrings (Owen, Isabella) have airtight alibis that are too easily verified, narrowing the suspect pool too quickly.
2. **Elias as a Camera:** Elias serves as a convenient narrative crutch to lock down the square's timeline.
3. **Linear Mid-game:** Once the player realizes Elias didn't see Clara at the fountain, the path to the end is a straight line.

### CONSENSUS
All reviewers agree the emotional core and mechanical fairness are excellent, but the difficulty is slightly too low because the red herrings are eliminated too efficiently.

### DISAGREEMENTS
The Detective thinks the mystery is slightly too easy; the Player and Screenwriter think the pacing is satisfying. I side with the Detective: a mystery this emotionally potent deserves a slightly more tangled middle act.

## EXECUTIVE DECISION

**Excellent**

**Explain:**
This is a beautifully constructed, emotionally resonant mystery. The interlocking timelines are mechanically flawless, and the motive is grounded in a tragic reality that elevates the entire narrative. I would fund this project and release it today, though I would strongly recommend implementing the "Quick Wins" to slightly dirty the alibis of the red herrings before final launch.

*   Would this mystery stand alongside the best modern detective games? Yes.
*   Would players remember it after one week? Yes.
*   Would players remember it after one month? Yes.
*   Would players remember it after one year? Yes, primarily for the emotional gut punch.
*   Would they recommend it to friends? Yes.
*   Would they replay it? Unlikely, but they would watch others play it.
*   Would streamers enjoy discovering it? Yes.

**"Why will players remember this mystery?"**
Players will remember this mystery because it subverts the classic "greedy employee" trope. When they finally corner Clara, there is no triumph—only the tragic realisation that a woman was driven to murder because a rigid man chose his ledgers over her mother's dignity. The mechanics are the hook, but the humanity is the anchor.
