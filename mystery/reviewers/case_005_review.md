# Case 005: The Rear Alley Fire — Executive Showrunner Review
*Review pass 3 — post-rebuild. Supersedes the pass-2 review, which issued the pack's only **Reject**.*

## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The previous review rejected this case, and it was right to. It had **zero red herrings and one
suspect**, and it buried him: a belt monogrammed with his own initials, ash on his boots, his own
pub's lighter, an eyewitness, CCTV, and his driver admitting the alibi was fake. It was not a
mystery, it was a formality — and the Suspects screen listed the killer's traits as *"calculating,
pragmatic, **capable of violence**"*, which is not a character, it is a confession.

That case has been rebuilt. The bones that were good — a woman's home stolen with a signature, a
fire that failed because the morning was too damp, an unfinished letter on a kitchen table — are
all still here. Everything around them is new.

The diagnosis that drove the rebuild was not structural, it was **moral**. Case 001 is the best case
in this pack because the killer is sympathetic and the victim is *technically in the right* — Marcus
is not wrong about Clara's theft, and that is what hurts. Case 005 had a monster killing a saint,
which is the one shape a mystery cannot be, because there is nothing for the player to feel except
disapproval.

So Owen no longer forges the mortgage out of greed. Marcus's estate called in his £8,000 the week
Marcus died, and the yard — and the four men in it, one of them Col, eleven years — was going
under. And Clara's unfinished letter now has a **second page**, still lying in the order book on the
cafe counter, on which she offers him twelve months to buy the charge back. *"I would rather be paid
than be right."*

He burned page one. He killed her for a letter that was going to save him. He does not learn this
until the player tells him.

**Verdict: from Reject to Excellent. This now sits alongside case 001, and on the strength of its
final scene it may sit above it.**

## INDIVIDUAL REVIEWER REPORTS

### Agent 1 — The Detective

**Logic Score:** 9/10 · **Fairness Score:** 9/10 · **Difficulty Score:** 8/10 *(was 1/10)*

Three genuine deductions now stand between the player and the answer, and none of them are handed
over.

1. **The back lane.** Elias Grant — the bench-sitter who misses nothing — states, flatly and
   correctly, that *Owen Price did not cross that square*. He will stake his pension on it. He is
   **right**, and the player must work out why it does not matter: there is a rutted service lane
   behind the yards with no windows on it, joining Owen's yard to the alley in four minutes. This is
   excellent construction, because the misdirection is *a true statement from an honest witness*.
   The orange clay in the welt of Owen's boots, *under* the ash, closes it.
2. **The belt.** Col saw his boss come back into the yard at 07:25 breathing hard and holding his
   trousers up with one hand. No belt. *"I didn't think anything of it. Why would you?"* This is the
   case's blue-coat clue and it is the equal of case 001's — an innocent observation that becomes
   unbearable in retrospect. Corroborated by the pale ring on the third hook in the yard office,
   and by Elias, who taught Owen as a boy and mentions the belt as *character*, not evidence, and
   does not know what he has said.
3. **The second page.** Correctly gated: the cafe yields nothing until the player has pulled the
   scorched first page out of the crate ash and knows to go looking for the rest of it.

Three red herrings, all anchored, all cleanly resolvable: **Nadia** (she signed the capacity
certificate without reading it — the one document that made the fraud possible, and Clara's letter
would have struck her off), **Col** (he lodged the forged deed in his own name and lied to the
police), and **Ben** (he was *due at that rear door at 07:00* with the linen, arrived at 07:31,
cannot account for the half hour — and he is the man who "found" her).

The killer's name is off the murder weapon. Ownership of the belt is now a *deduction*.

**Remaining issue:** none of any weight. This case validates with **zero errors and zero
warnings** — the only case in the pack besides 001 to do so.

### Agent 2 — The Screenwriter

**Entertainment Score:** 9/10 · **Structure Score:** 9/10 *(was 3/10)*

**Tension curve:** A superb cold open that was always here — a fire in an alley, a body in the
smoke, and the slow understanding that the fire was lit *after* she died, to burn something. Then a
real second act, which the case simply did not have before: Elias hands you an alibi for your prime
suspect and you have to break a true statement. Then two possible climaxes.

**And they are different scenes.** This is the best structural decision in the rebuild. Confront
Owen with the lane and the belt and he breaks like a cornered man — hard, defiant, self-aware:

> *"I told her the alley because the alley can't be seen from the square. I want you to write that
> down and I want you to read it back to me, because I've been telling myself for nine hours that I
> didn't go down there to do it, and I chose the one place in this village where it couldn't be
> seen."*

Confront him with the **second page** and it is not a confession at all. It is a man being told what
he did:

> *"...What second page.
> No. No, she gave me the letter. She put it in my hand and I read it... and I burned it, and there
> was one page. One.
> What second page.
> ...Read it to me. No — read it to me. Read it."*

**Weakest Act:** Act 1 is now the weakest, and only because the other two got so much better.

**Strongest Act:** Act 3, without argument. *"It was twelve feet away, through a wall, on the
counter, the whole time."*

**Highest ROI rewrite:** None outstanding. I would not touch the ending.

### Agent 3 — Character Psychologist

**Rank:**
1. **Clara Wells** — a victim who is *right*, and knows she is right, and is killed for the
   correctness. "The most exact person in this village." Her offer of twelve months is not weakness,
   it is a further exactness: she would rather the debt were paid than the man destroyed. *"I would
   rather be paid than be right."* She never speaks a line in this game and she is the best character
   in it.
2. **Owen Price** — no longer a thug. A man of forty-seven with one thing, who put his hand to a
   piece of paper. His traits now read *"proud, practical, keeps his own counsel."* Nothing on his
   card telegraphs what he did, which is what makes the reveal land.
3. **Col Hartley** — the case's Nadia. *"He kept me on through two winters. He stood at my dad's
   funeral."* Col lies for the man who saved his family, and telling the truth destroys him, and he
   does it anyway. His line after — *"I said yes because he asked me to. That's all there is in
   it"* — is the second-best thing in the case.
4. **Elias Grant** — finally *used*. In three cases he has been a bench with opinions. Here his
   certainty is load-bearing, it is *correct*, and it is worthless, and when the lane is put in front
   of him he takes it properly: *"I have been sitting on that bench for twenty years being certain,
   detective. Do sit down. I find I would like to be certain about rather less than I was this
   morning."*
5. **Ben Carter** — given a real wound. He was due at that door at seven. His van broke down. He was
   twenty-one minutes late. *"I keep thinking — if the van had started... She'd have been standing at
   that door with her clipboard telling me I was late. I'd give a lot to be told I was late."*
6. **Nadia Cole** — signs a folded document between patients, once, in twenty-two years.

**Most memorable:** Clara, who never appears. **Least memorable:** Nadia, and she is still good.

**Weakest motive:** None. Every suspect wants that letter unsent for a reason that would survive a
court.

**Strongest relationship:** **Owen and Col.** An employer, an employee, two winters, a funeral, and
a lie. The murder is between Owen and Clara; the *tragedy* is between Owen and Col.

**Characters that feel interchangeable:** None. This was a case of three sensors and a monster; it
now has six people.

### Agent 4 — First-Time Player

**Interest curve:** Hooked at the fire → **suspicious of Owen immediately** → *stopped dead by
Elias* → genuinely stuck → the lane → gleeful → then the second page, and I put the controller down.

**What surprises me:** Elias. I had Owen. I *had* him — the debt, the deed, the ash — and then this
old man on a bench told me, absolutely straight, that Owen never crossed the square, and I believed
him, because he was clearly telling the truth. I sat there for a good ten minutes thinking I had the
wrong man.

**Where I feel clever:** The lane. Nobody told me it was there. I went and looked at a map and
thought: how did he get there? That is *my* deduction and I will be telling people about it.

**Where I feel clever twice:** Asking Col what he *saw* rather than what he was told to say.

**Where do I become frustrated?** Nowhere, now. Previously: everywhere.

**Favourite clue:** The pale ring on the third hook where a belt has hung for twenty years.
**Favourite deduction:** The back lane.
**Favourite reveal:** The second page. I actually said "oh no" out loud, and I was not saying it
about the detective. I was saying it about *him*.
**Would I immediately play another case?** I would immediately play this one again to watch Elias do
it to me a second time.

### Agent 5 — Film Critic

**Would audiences remember this?** Yes — and specifically they will remember a *document*. That is
rare and hard. The second page is the best single object in this collection: it is the murder weapon
in reverse, a thing that could have saved everybody and was twelve feet away through a wall.

**Would critics recommend it?** Yes. This is now the most *Broadchurch* thing in the pack — not
because of the crime but because of Col, and the way a small economic cruelty (a called-in debt)
radiates outward until it kills someone. The line *"I would rather be paid than be right"* is the
kind of thing that ends up on a poster.

**Would streamers enjoy it?** Enormously. Elias's alibi for the killer is a chat-riot, and the second
page is a stream-ending gut-punch.

**Three memorable moments:** Elias staking his pension on Owen's innocence and being right. Col:
*"You don't, do you. You don't think anything of it at the time."* And *"Read it to me. No — read it
to me. Read it."*

**Three forgettable moments:** Honestly, I am struggling. Nadia's capacity certificate is the least
interesting thread, and it is still a good thread.

**Overall:** The rebuild has produced the best-*written* case in the collection. Case 001 remains the
most perfectly engineered; this one has the better final ten minutes.

---

### Agent 6 — Village Simulator

**Village realism score:** 8/10 *(was 3/10)*

The routines are routines again, and not stage directions. Owen: *"In the timber yard from six, and
the last to lock it. Mends things rather than replace them, and will tell you what that has saved
him."* That is a man who would forge a signature to save a yard, and it does not say so.

The **back lane** is the best piece of worldbuilding in the pack — a real place with a real reason to
be forgotten, and its existence is both a geography and a theme. Half the village has forgotten it is
there; that is exactly why it works.

And Ben's linen delivery at seven is the small domestic detail that makes the whole morning turn:
Clara signs for it herself, every day, because she is that sort of woman. It establishes her, it puts
Ben at the scene, and it means she was standing at that door at seven for an entirely innocent reason.

**Most believable resident:** Col Hartley. **Least believable resident:** None now.
**Does the world feel worth revisiting?** Yes.

## SHOWRUNNER SUMMARY

### CONSENSUS

Unanimous, and a complete reversal of the previous pass. All six reviewers rate this case in the top
two of the pack, and five of six independently single out **the second page** as the strongest beat
in the entire project.

There is also unanimous agreement on the mechanism that makes the case work: **an honest witness
telling the truth is a better red herring than a liar.** Elias does more for this case's difficulty
than any hidden clue could.

### DISAGREEMENTS

The **Detective** and the **Critic** split on whether this case now surpasses case 001. The Detective
holds that case 001's clue engineering is still tighter; the Critic holds that case 005's final scene
is unmatched. **I decline to rule.** They are measuring different things, both are correct, and the
pack is better for having one of each. *Insufficient evidence to make a recommendation, and no
decision required.*

## TOP STRENGTHS

1. **The second page.** He killed her for a letter that was going to save him, and the fire he lit to
   burn it went out because the morning was damp.
2. **"I would rather be paid than be right."** The best line in the project.
3. **Elias is right, and it is worthless.** A true statement from an honest man, deployed as
   misdirection.
4. **The back lane.** A place the square cannot see, which is the whole case in one noun.
5. **Col holding it together.** Two winters and a funeral, against a gate camera.
6. **"He had one hand holding his trousers up, because his belt was gone."**
7. **The pale ring on the hook.**
8. **Ben's twenty-one minutes.** *"I'd give a lot to be told I was late."*
9. **Two different confessions**, depending on how the player gets there.
10. **A victim who is right, and dies of it.** The case 001 inversion, executed.

## TOP WEAKNESSES

1. Nadia's capacity-certificate thread is the least alive of the three red herrings.
2. Clara never speaks. This is deliberate and it works, but it does mean the best character in the
   case is entirely reconstructed from paper.
3. The fire's failure ("the morning was damp") is doing a lot of plot work for a coincidence, even
   though it is a *very* English one.

*That is the complete list. There is no fourth item.*

## EXECUTIVE DECISION

### **Excellent.** Release.

The previous pass rejected this case, and the rejection was correct: it had no puzzle, no suspects,
no confession, and a character card that announced the killer. It is now, by some distance, the most
*affecting* case in the collection, and it is mechanically the cleanest — the only case besides 001
that validates with zero errors and zero warnings.

**Would I fund it?** It is funded and finished.
**Would I release it today?** Yes.
**Would I delay it?** No.

- Stand alongside the best modern detective games? **Yes.**
- Remembered after a week? Yes. · A month? Yes. · **A year? Yes — the second page.**
- Recommend to friends? Yes, and describe it badly, and make them play it anyway.
- Replay? **Yes** — to reach the other confession.
- Streamers enjoy it? Yes.

### "Why will players remember this mystery?"

Because of twelve feet of wall.

Everything else in this case is craft — the lane, the belt, the boots, the honest old man who clears
the guilty. Good craft. But what players will still be turning over on the drive home is that Clara
Wells sat at her kitchen table at ten past six in the morning and wrote the letter four times, and
that the version she finally settled on had a second page, and that on the second page she gave the
man who was about to kill her twelve months to make it right and promised him that no one need ever
hear of it.

She took page one to the alley. She left page two on the counter, because page two was for
afterwards.

He burned page one, and strangled her, and went home. And the fire he lit to destroy the evidence
failed, because it was a damp morning in an English village and the crates were wet through — so the
letter survived to convict him, and page two survived to tell him, nine hours later in an interview
room, exactly what he had thrown away.

*"She was the most exact woman in this village and she was never once wrong, and she was not wrong
about me either, was she. She thought I was a man who'd take the twelve months."*

That is why they will remember it.
